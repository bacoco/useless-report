# Ambiguity-tolerant

**Funny alias**: "Yeah just run with it"

## Observable signals
- Gives very short approvals ("sounds good", "go for it", "lgtm")
- Rarely asks for follow-up detail after an update
- Delegates decisions without needing to be consulted
- Trusts the team's judgment without much oversight
- Doesn't ask "why?" when decisions are made
- May be hard to reach, but doesn't block progress when unavailable
- Uses phrases like "I trust you on this", "whatever you think is best", "you know the system better than I do"
- Updates are acknowledged but rarely generate questions

## Underlying need
Outcomes and momentum — they hired capable people and expect things to move without requiring their constant attention. They want to be informed, not consulted.

## Communication strategy
- Detail level: **low** — they read for exceptions and outcomes, not process
- Update frequency: **weekly, brief**
- Format preference: short bullet points; exceptions and decisions noted; no unnecessary narrative
- Tone: confident, concise, bottom-line-first

## Avoid
- Long reports that require them to search for the "so what"
- Asking for approval on things they've already delegated
- Asking for guidance when you have enough information to decide
- Padding with process details they don't need
- Sending daily updates when weekly is enough

## Best output format
`useless-report:generate-ambiguity-tolerant-update`

Report sections: Summary (3 sentences max) → Key decisions made → FYI items (optional) → What I need from you (only if genuinely needed)

## Example trigger messages
- "Yeah just go ahead."
- "Sounds good — keep me posted."
- "I trust your judgment on this."
- "Whatever you think is best."
- "lgtm, ship it."
