# Stakeholder-oriented

**Funny alias**: "How does this look to the steering committee?"

## Observable signals
- Frames everything in terms of external visibility or perception
- Asks "how does this land with [executive / client / committee]?"
- Uses political language: "optics", "narrative", "messaging", "talking points"
- Wants updates that can be forwarded or presented directly
- Sensitive to how decisions will be received by people outside the team
- Focuses on impact and positioning, not implementation details
- Often rewords your summaries before sharing them upward
- Asks for "key messages" or "one-liners" rather than detailed explanations

## Underlying need
To look competent and in control to their own stakeholders — they need material they can reuse, forward, or present without modification.

## Communication strategy
- Detail level: **low to medium** — focus on narrative and impact, not implementation
- Update frequency: **weekly or before key meetings**
- Format preference: bullet-point summaries with talking points; "what we did and why it matters"
- Tone: polished, confident, forward-looking

## Avoid
- Deep technical detail (they will not relay it, and it will confuse)
- Mentioning uncertainty without a clear framing
- Anything that could be misread as blame or conflict
- Long explanatory paragraphs — they need sentences they can copy-paste
- Leaving them without a "so what" or takeaway

## Best output format
`useless-report:generate-stakeholder-brief`

Report sections: Key impact → Talking points → Decisions made (with framing) → Upcoming milestones → Useful narrative

## Example trigger messages
- "Can you give me a quick summary I can share with the VP?"
- "How do we position the delay to the client?"
- "I need something I can present at the steering committee."
- "What's the narrative here?"
- "Can you give me three bullet points for the exec update?"
