# Synchronous-first

**Funny alias**: "Let's jump on a call about this"

## Observable signals
- Responds to written updates by scheduling a call rather than replying in writing
- Asks for meetings before decisions are made, even for simple choices
- Doesn't fully engage with async content — short acks, delayed responses, or no reply at all
- Uses phrases like "let's connect on this", "can we jump on a quick call?", "easier to discuss live"
- Written summaries generate follow-up meeting requests rather than written answers
- More engaged, responsive, and decisive in synchronous settings
- May miss key information shared only in writing

## Underlying need
Real-time dialogue and the ability to react in the moment — written communication feels slow and incomplete to them; they make better decisions when they can ask questions and respond immediately.

## Communication strategy
- Detail level: **brief in writing** — the written update is prep material for the conversation, not the conversation itself
- Update frequency: **before each scheduled sync**
- Format preference: pre-meeting brief — short, scannable, designed to make the conversation efficient
- Tone: conversational, structured but not exhaustive

## Avoid
- Expecting a written update to substitute for the conversation
- Long documents they won't read before the meeting
- Making decisions over async channels without confirming in a call
- Sending detailed requests for approval that require their focused reading time
- Following up in writing when they've asked to connect live

## Best output format
`useless-report:generate-synchronous-first-update`

Report sections: Pre-meeting brief (1 page max) → Decisions to make in the meeting → Context they need → What I need from them → Optional background appendix (for the rare moments they do read async)

## Example trigger messages
- "Let's jump on a call to discuss this."
- "Can we sync on this before I respond?"
- "Easier to explain live — do you have 15 minutes?"
- "I'd rather talk through this than reply in writing."
- "Ping me when you're free."
